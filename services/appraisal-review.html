Appraisal Review & Second Opinions

A formal, standards-based review of another appraiser's report when the value or the method is in question.

Sometimes the question is not what a property is worth, but whether someone else's appraisal can be relied on. A formal appraisal review examines another appraiser's data, methods and reasoning and reports whether the conclusions are credible — the kind of evidence an attorney can use, rather than an informal opinion.

When a review makes sense

Opposing appraisals in a divorce or partition case, an estate appraisal a beneficiary believes is too low, a report that seems to ignore obvious comparable sales, or a value that has drifted far from what the market suggests are the typical triggers.

What the review covers

We check the report's data against public records and the MLS, test whether the comparable sales were the most relevant ones available, evaluate each adjustment, and look for internal inconsistencies and compliance issues. Reviews are developed and reported under the appraisal review standard in USPAP.

Review with a second value

If the reviewer disagrees with the original conclusion, the assignment can be expanded to include our own opinion of value as of the same effective date. We will tell you which scope fits your purpose when we quote the work.
