The San Mateo County housing landscape

Dense postwar tracts of attached and detached homes cover Daly City, South San Francisco and San Bruno, while Burlingame, San Mateo and Belmont mix pre-war period homes with mid-century neighborhoods, including the Eichler homes of the San Mateo Highlands. Foster City and Redwood Shores were built on bay fill as lagoon communities, with many homes on the water. Hillsborough, Atherton, Woodside and Portola Valley are zoned for large residential lots, and Woodside and Portola Valley add horse properties and semi-rural estates. On the coast, Half Moon Bay, El Granada, Montara and Pacifica pair ocean-view homes with farmland, nurseries and open space.

Valuation considerations

Bluff erosion in Pacifica has forced the demolition of buildings at the ocean's edge, and coastside parcels must account for Coastal Commission review and setback limits. Foster City's levees and Redwood Shores' lagoons raise flood-zone and sea-level questions, while homes within San Francisco International Airport's noise contours in South San Francisco, San Bruno and Millbrae carry an aircraft-noise influence. Hillside parcels near the San Andreas Fault and in Pacifica's canyons can involve landslide and fault-zone disclosures.

Is a desktop appraisal the right fit?

Dense bayside sales in Daly City, San Mateo and Redwood City give a desktop appraisal a solid footing for estate, trust and divorce assignments. Bluff-top coastal homes, estate properties in Woodside or Portola Valley with outbuildings, and hillside homes with unpermitted additions are stronger candidates for an inspection.
