The Stanislaus County housing landscape

Modesto's older neighborhoods near downtown and the College Area have pre-war and mid-century homes on tree-lined streets, while postwar ranch tracts and newer subdivisions spread north and east. Turlock, Ceres, Riverbank and Oakdale grew through newer tract development, and Patterson on the west side draws commuters who cross the Altamont Pass to Bay Area jobs. Outside city limits, homes on small acreage with almond or walnut orchards, private wells and septic systems are common, and Del Rio, north of Modesto, is an unincorporated community built around a country club.

Valuation considerations

Agricultural and rural parcels must be analyzed for surface-water service from the Modesto, Turlock or Oakdale irrigation districts, groundwater limits under the state's sustainable groundwater law, and any Williamson Act contract, since water access can drive land value as much as the house itself. Homes near the Tuolumne and Stanislaus rivers can fall within mapped flood zones, and manufactured homes on owned land need to be distinguished from those in parks and from site-built comparables.

Is a desktop appraisal the right fit?

Modesto and Turlock tract homes, which sell often and in similar form, are a natural fit for desktop valuation in estate, trust or divorce work. Orchard parcels, rural homesites with wells and outbuildings, and manufactured homes on land should be inspected so that land, water and improvements are each valued correctly.
