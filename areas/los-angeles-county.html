The Los Angeles County housing landscape

Pre-war Craftsman, Spanish Revival and Tudor homes are concentrated in Pasadena, Glendale, Long Beach and older Los Angeles neighborhoods, while Torrance, Downey and much of the San Fernando Valley filled in with single-story tracts after World War II. Hillside houses climb the Hollywood Hills, the Santa Monica Mountains and the Verdugo foothills above Glendale and Burbank, and duplexes, courtyard buildings and older condominiums are woven through Hollywood, Inglewood and Santa Monica. Santa Clarita, Lancaster and Palmdale supply newer master-planned subdivisions, often on larger lots, for buyers who trade commute time for space. With dozens of incorporated cities packed side by side, housing age, rent rules and buyer demand can change at a city line, and comparable sales are usually drawn from within the same city or community.

Valuation considerations

Retrospective date-of-death work tied to the January 2025 Palisades and Eaton fires turns on the effective date: a death before the fires calls for valuing the intact home in the pre-fire market, while a later date may involve a burned or cleared lot in Pacific Palisades or Altadena and a changed insurance landscape. Older apartments and duplexes in the City of Los Angeles and Santa Monica can fall under local rent control, which shapes income and the buyer pool for occupied units. Hillside drainage and retaining walls, active landslide ground such as Portuguese Bend in Rancho Palos Verdes, and aircraft noise beneath the LAX approach over Inglewood also call for site-specific analysis.

Is a desktop appraisal the right fit?

Where records are clean and same-tract sales are plentiful, as with tract homes in Torrance, Downey or Palmdale and condominiums in Long Beach, a desktop report meets the needs of a typical estate, trust or divorce assignment. Parcels in the burn areas, hillside homes with unpermitted additions, and income properties with undocumented interiors are better served by an in-person inspection.
