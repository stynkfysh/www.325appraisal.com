Independent appraisals for estates, trusts, divorce, bankruptcy, taxes and litigation — in person or by desktop.

Between Cathedral City and Palm Desert, Rancho Mirage grew from private country clubs laid out along Highway 111 and Frank Sinatra Drive beginning in the 1950s. It remains a city of guard-gated golf communities, hillside estates and resort-style condominiums, with a market shaped by winter residents and second-home owners.

The Rancho Mirage housing market

Thunderbird and Tamarisk, both from the early 1950s, hold large mid-century homes on generous fairway lots, while Mission Hills, Morningside, The Springs, Sunrise and Rancho Las Palmas added condominiums and single-family homes mostly in the 1970s and 1980s. Smaller gated enclaves such as Magnesia Falls Cove and Thunderbird Heights sit at the base of the mountains with view lots and custom construction, and Mirada Estates occupies the hillside above Highway 111. Del Webb Rancho Mirage brought a more recent age-restricted option to the city.

What shapes value in Rancho Mirage

Leased Agua Caliente land underlies parts of the city, and the city sharply restricts short-term vacation rentals, so neither lease terms nor rental income can be borrowed from a neighboring sale. Among the clubs, some require equity or social memberships with initiation fees while others leave golf optional, and that structure changes what a buyer actually receives with the deed. Hillside and cove lots are adjusted for view quality and privacy and reviewed for drainage from the mountain canyons.

Around Rancho Mirage

Sunnylands, the former Annenberg estate now open to the public as a center and gardens, and the Eisenhower Health medical campus are defining institutions, joined by The River shopping and dining center and the Agua Caliente casino resort. The city library, with its public observatory, serves as a civic gathering place.
