The San Francisco housing landscape

Victorian and Edwardian houses and flats fill neighborhoods such as Noe Valley, the Haight, Pacific Heights and the Mission, and many single-family Victorians were divided into units over the decades. The Sunset and Parkside, along with much of the outer Richmond and the Excelsior, were built out with stucco row houses over ground-floor garages in the years before and after World War II. Condominium towers and mid-rise buildings dominate SoMa, Mission Bay and Rincon Hill, and tenancy-in-common ownership is common in older multi-unit buildings because condominium conversion is restricted. View homes on Twin Peaks and in Diamond Heights form a separate hillside market.

Valuation considerations

Units in buildings built on or before June 13, 1979 are generally covered by the city's rent ordinance, so an occupied flat or multi-unit building must be valued with its tenancy in mind, and tenancy-in-common interests need comparables that reflect fractional financing and conversion status. Many older wood-frame apartment buildings fell under the mandatory soft-story seismic retrofit program, and whether that work was completed is a value question. Liquefaction zones in the Marina, SoMa and Mission Bay, and historic-district rules in places such as Alamo Square and Liberty Hill, add further layers.

Is a desktop appraisal the right fit?

For an estate, trust or property-tax matter, a Sunset row house or a condominium in a building with regular unit sales can usually be valued from records, photographs and comparable sales without a site visit. An occupied multi-unit building, a Victorian with unpermitted in-law units, or a tenancy-in-common share with unclear unit details warrants an interior inspection.
