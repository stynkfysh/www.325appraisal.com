The Santa Ynez Mountains split Santa Barbara County in two: Carpinteria, Montecito, Santa Barbara and Goleta face the ocean on the south coast, while the Santa Ynez Valley, Lompoc and Santa Maria lie inland and to the north. The two halves differ in housing stock, buyers and hazards, and sales on one side rarely serve as comparables for the other.

The Santa Barbara County housing landscape

Along the south coast, mountains, ocean and long-standing growth controls restrict new building, so sales mainly involve existing homes, from Spanish Colonial Revival houses downtown and on the Riviera to mid-century tracts in Goleta and on the Mesa. Montecito and Hope Ranch, the latter a private community with its own homes association and roads, hold estate properties on large lots. Isla Vista's dense rentals serve the university, while the Santa Ynez Valley around Solvang, Los Olivos and Santa Ynez has ranches, horse properties and vineyards. Santa Maria and Lompoc have more conventional tract neighborhoods tied to agriculture and Vandenberg Space Force Base.

Valuation considerations

The January 2018 Montecito debris flows, which followed the Thomas Fire, made debris-flow hazard part of the analysis for homes below the foothill canyons, and wildfire exposure and insurance availability weigh on Riviera and foothill properties. Bluff-top homes on the Mesa and in Isla Vista face erosion and Coastal Commission constraints, and much of downtown Santa Barbara falls under historic design review. Vineyard and ranch parcels in the Santa Ynez Valley often carry Williamson Act contracts and depend on well water, both of which influence value.

Is a desktop appraisal the right fit?

Goleta tracts and Santa Maria subdivisions, with steady resale activity, often lend themselves to a desktop appraisal for estate or trust purposes. Montecito estates, Riviera view homes and Santa Ynez Valley ranches vary too much in quality, view and site features to value confidently without an inspection.
