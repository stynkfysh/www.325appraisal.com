Spring Valley Home Appraisals

Independent appraisals for estates, trusts, divorce, bankruptcy, taxes and litigation — in person or by desktop.

Named for the natural spring that drew early settlers and later became part of historian Hubert Howe Bancroft's ranch, Spring Valley is an unincorporated community southeast of Lemon Grove and La Mesa. Its neighborhoods fill the valley and climb surrounding hills such as Dictionary Hill, with Sweetwater Reservoir and the foothills toward Jamul to the south and east.

The Spring Valley housing market

Construction peaked between the 1950s and the 1980s, producing everything from small ranch houses on the valley floor to hillside homes with views and larger lots, some of which allow horses and other animals. Casa de Oro to the north and the Rancho San Diego edge to the east add later tracts and planned communities, while apartments and mobile home parks line corridors such as Jamacha Boulevard and Sweetwater Road. Buyers often weigh Spring Valley against Lemon Grove, La Mesa and eastern Chula Vista, usually in search of more land than those areas offer.

What shapes value in Spring Valley

Because Spring Valley is unincorporated, zoning, ADU rules and permits fall under the County, and lot sizes and animal-keeping rights can differ sharply between neighboring streets. Steep hillside lots on Dictionary Hill and similar slopes need adjustment for access, grade and usable area as well as view, and older homes often carry additions whose permit history should be confirmed. Mobile homes on owned land and those on rented park spaces are valued on different footings, a distinction that comes up often here.

Around Spring Valley

The Bancroft Ranch House, an adobe designated a National Historic Landmark, preserves the area's earliest history, and open space on Dictionary Hill and around Sweetwater Reservoir frames the community. Monte Vista, Mount Miguel and Steele Canyon high schools in the Grossmont Union district serve local students.
