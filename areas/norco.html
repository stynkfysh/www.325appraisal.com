Independent appraisals for estates, trusts, divorce, bankruptcy, taxes and litigation — in person or by desktop.

Norco takes its name from the North Corona Land Company, through which developer Rex Clark opened the town in 1923 as a community of small farms. That heritage survives in the animal-keeping zones that cover most of its residential land, and since incorporating in 1964 the city has embraced its Horsetown USA identity, with riding trails in place of sidewalks along many streets.

The Norco housing market

Single-story ranch homes from the 1950s through the 1970s, often on lots of about half an acre, make up the core of the housing stock and are frequently improved with barns, corrals, arenas and tack rooms. Larger homes were added on the hillsides in later decades, and a smaller number of tracts have conventional suburban lots. Equestrians and families who want animal-keeping rights close to the 15 and 91 freeways dominate the buyer pool, and listings with usable flat land and trail access attract particular interest. The city charter requires a four-fifths council vote to change lot-size and animal-keeping rules, which reinforces the market's equestrian character.

What shapes value in Norco

Comparable selection turns on usable lot area, flat versus sloped ground, trail frontage and the condition of equestrian improvements, which buyers weigh differently from conventional suburban features. Guest quarters and converted outbuildings are common, so permit status and ADU legality need verification. Parcels on the river side of the city should be checked against FEMA flood maps.

Around Norco

A trail network links neighborhoods to the Santa Ana River bottom, and the George Ingalls Equestrian Event Center hosts shows and community events. Norco College, the Naval Surface Warfare Center on the grounds of the former Lake Norconian Club and the California Rehabilitation Center are the city's major institutions.
