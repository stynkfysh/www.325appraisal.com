Del Mar incorporated in 1959 to hold on to its small-town scale and keep high-rises off its shoreline, and almost the entire city lies within the Coastal Zone. Beach Colony cottages and condominiums, village bungalows and hillside view houses make up the housing stock, with the fairgrounds and the San Dieguito River at the north end.

The Del Mar housing market

Olde Del Mar, the historic core around Camino Del Mar, has small and sometimes irregular lots with early twentieth-century bungalows, Tudor Revival touches and rebuilds sized to their parcels. The Beach Colony mixes oceanfront single-family homes with condominium and townhome buildings, many governed by associations, while the hillside streets and Rancho Del Mar offer larger lots with ranch homes, Mediterranean remodels and newer custom construction. Many owners are second-home buyers or relocating households, and with few sales in a given year, each one carries weight in the local picture. Del Mar Heights, part of the City of San Diego, shares the Del Mar mailing address and competes for many of the same buyers.

What shapes value in Del Mar

Bluff erosion along the south beach, the rail line that runs atop those bluffs and the city's choice of seawalls and sand replenishment over managed retreat all bear on bluff-top values. Most exterior changes require a coastal development permit along with design review, which affects the cost and feasibility of remodels and rebuilds. Short-term rentals are now limited to a capped, owner-occupied permit program, and homes on the low-lying streets near the river mouth call for a check of flood-zone mapping.

Around Del Mar

Powerhouse Community Center, Seagrove Park and Del Mar City Beach anchor the village, while the Del Mar Fairgrounds and racetrack, home of the San Diego County Fair, occupy the river flats at the north end beside San Dieguito Lagoon. Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve and State Beach border the city to the south.
