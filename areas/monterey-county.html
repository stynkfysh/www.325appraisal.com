The Monterey County housing landscape

Salinas has older homes around Oldtown, postwar tracts, and newer subdivisions on its north and east sides, and the valley towns of Gonzales, Soledad, Greenfield and King City add modest homes and farm parcels. On the Peninsula, Pacific Grove is known for Victorian cottages, Carmel-by-the-Sea for storybook cottages on small lots, and Pebble Beach and Carmel Valley for estate homes, golf frontage, ranches and vineyards. Former Fort Ord land in Marina and Seaside, along with East Garrison, has been redeveloped with new subdivisions. Valley demand follows the agricultural economy, while the Peninsula draws retirees, second-home owners, and households connected to the Naval Postgraduate School and the Defense Language Institute.

Valuation considerations

Scarce water connections on the Monterey Peninsula have long constrained new construction and additions, so an existing water entitlement can carry real weight in value. Big Sur parcels face Coastal Commission and local coastal plan limits, landslide-related highway closures and wildfire exposure, while Salinas Valley farmland must be analyzed for water supply, crop use and any Williamson Act contract. Newer homes on former Fort Ord land may carry special taxes and assessments that older Salinas or Seaside homes do not.

Is a desktop appraisal the right fit?

Salinas tract homes and newer houses on former Fort Ord land usually have enough comparable sales to support a desktop appraisal in an estate, trust or divorce matter. Carmel cottages, Big Sur coastal parcels and Salinas Valley farm properties are individual enough that an inspection is often the wiser choice.
